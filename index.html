Introduction

Water is essential — but it isn't priced or traded like it.

Water prices vary widely across regions, quality, and use case. Despite being vital to agriculture, industry, and human survival, water is not yet consistently priced or traded as a global commodity.

Why a market?

A market-based price changes how the world uses water.

Pricing water as a commodity incentivizes producers to allocate resources responsibly — and unlocks capital, conservation, and investment in infrastructure.

Price discovery

A transparent, market-based price gives every stakeholder a common reference for the value of water.

Responsible allocation

Markets route scarce water to where it is valued most, correcting decades of inefficient appropriation.

Conservation

When water carries a defined economic value, waste in production processes falls sharply.

Collateralization

Recognized value lets reserve holders borrow against water without selling the underlying resource.

Real world asset

Institutions gain a new, inflation-resilient asset that serves as a benchmark across agricultural markets.

Deliverability

Collect fresh water at delivery points by truck or by pipeline.

Collateralization

Unlock the value held in fresh-water reserves.

Many governments, landowners, and institutions control large quantities of fresh water that have never been drawn upon as an asset of known value. AquaIndex assigns that water a recognized value — so reserves can be collateralized to access capital, fund infrastructure, and drive growth, without selling the underlying resource.

The Benchmark

Patented pricing methodology and water indices.

AquaIndex extracts an impartial price for water from the transparent prices of agricultural and other commodities, and from the quality and mineral content of physical sources — the foundation for indices and benchmarks.

  • Impartial pricing algorithms derived from transparent global commodity markets
  • A virtual water index — extracted from agricultural futures, water footprint, and yield
  • Physical water valuation by verified quality and mineral content
  • Standards and specifications for a water-based futures contract
  • Patented rights to use valuation pricing for collateralization
